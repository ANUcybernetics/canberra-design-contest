# Canberra’s 1911–12 design competition: a guide to the entries and their images

Research completed 27 September 2026. The links below favour images of the actual submitted plans and drawings. **Original** means a contemporary competition drawing or plan; **copy** means a period or later photograph, print, or scan of a competition entry; **reconstruction** means a modern interpretation. Some catalogue links establish where an object is held but do not display the whole object online.

## Where the surviving designs are

The competition received **137 entries**. The National Archives of Australia (NAA) holds original drawings for the Griffins and three other leading submissions in **series A710**, and photographic copies of additional unsuccessful entries in **series A763**. The National Library of Australia (NLA) holds photographic copies of competition entries. The 1995 *An Ideal City?* exhibition showed **46 surviving shortlisted designs**; John W. Reps’s *Canberra 1912* reproduces 47 preserved plans. These counts concern different objects and presentations and should not be read as 137 complete entries available online. [NAA collection guide](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra); [NLA collection guide](https://www.library.gov.au/research/guides-and-resources/guides-selected-collections/griffin-and-early-canberra-collection); [exhibition record](https://mhnsw.au/whats-on/exhibitions/ideal-city-1912-competition-design-canberra/); [Reps book record](https://books.google.com/books/about/Canberra_1912.html?id=elPo7ti8zNoC).

**How to get more:** In NAA RecordSearch, search series **A710** for original finalists and **A763** for photographic copies; in NLA/Trove, search an entrant’s name together with *Federal Capital Design Competition*. RecordSearch’s old deep links can expire, so the [NAA guide](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra) is the more dependable entry point. The old collaborative *An Ideal City?* site (`idealcity.org.au/competition-3-shortlist-46.html`, with entrant pages of the form `/46/shortlist-18.html`) was cited by ANU Archives and the NAA/NLA but was not loading when checked for this report. Its [book/site description](https://books.google.com/books/about/An_Ideal_City.html?id=81ByvwEACAAJ) says that Reps interpreted all 46 designs. Avoid treating a 1913 or 1918 revision of Griffin’s plan as the original competition entry.

## Entry 29 — Walter Burley Griffin and Marion Mahony Griffin (first prize)

- [NAA’s *Design 29* online exhibition](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/ngWxSQeb56c3KA) — Displays several actual competition images, including *City and Environs*, a central district plan, sectional panels, and Marion’s panorama from Mount Ainslie, with captions explaining the drawings.
- [NAA: *View from the Summit of Mount Ainslie*](https://www.naa.gov.au/students-and-teachers/student-research-portal/learning-resource-themes/government-and-democracy/federation/walter-burley-griffins-plan-canberra-view-summit-mount-ainslie) — Shows and describes the original three-panel hand-coloured perspective, explicitly identifying it as part of entry 29.
- [Google Arts & Culture: the Mount Ainslie drawing](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/view-from-summit-of-mount-ainslie-marion-mahony-griffin/iAEHR8v1GBTNtg?hl=en) — An individual zoomable image record for Marion’s perspective drawing (NAA A710/48).
- [NAA: *Plan for Canberra*](https://www.naa.gov.au/students-and-teachers/learning-resources/learning-resource-themes/government-and-democracy/parliament-and-elections/plan-canberra) — Presents one of Marion’s competition drawings with a brief explanation of the axes, lake and topography.
- [NAA: *Federal Capital Design No 29*](https://www.naa.gov.au/visit-us/events-and-exhibitions/marion-other-griffin/federal-capital-design-no-29) — Identifies the Griffins’ original accompanying competition report as A710/52 and shows its cover; useful for locating the written submission.
- [Wikimedia Commons: plan drawn on the competition contour map](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canberra_plan-WBG.jpg) — A freely viewable 2,953 × 2,920-pixel reproduction of the submitted layout on its supplied base map.
- [Wikimedia Commons: *City and Environs*](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canberra_plan_shaded-WBG.jpg) — A 1,494 × 2,952-pixel reproduction of the famous coloured competition plan; the NAA exhibition above supplies the archival context.
- [Wikimedia Commons: Mount Ainslie panorama](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canberra_plan_view-WBG.jpg) — A wide 2,952 × 804-pixel copy of the submitted panoramic perspective.
- [NLA: Griffin and early Canberra collection](https://www.library.gov.au/research/guides-and-resources/guides-selected-collections/griffin-and-early-canberra-collection) — Points to competition photographs, slides, watercolours and an offprint of *City and Environs* among later plans; distinguish the later plans by date.

The design was submitted under Walter’s name, while Marion produced the celebrated presentation drawings. The NAA calls the extent of collaboration uncertain but her contribution substantial. [NAA exhibition](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/ngWxSQeb56c3KA).

## Entry 18 — Eliel Saarinen, with Frans Nyberg (second prize)

- [Finnish Architecture Museum: seven highlights of Saarinen’s collection](https://www.mfa.fi/uutiskirjeet/seitseman-nostoa-eliel-saarisen-kokoelmasta/) — Includes an image of Saarinen’s **1912 Canberra perspective study** and curator commentary in Finnish; use “Katso kuva isompana” beneath the Canberra passage to enlarge it.
- [Finnish Architecture and Design Museum’s Saarinen collection](https://mfa.finna.fi/Content/elielsaarinen) — Introduces its digitised original drawings and explicitly identifies sketches for the Canberra second-prize plan; search within the collection for “Canberran asemakaavakilpailu.”
- [Finna catalogue citation for the Canberra perspective sketch](https://www.finna.fi/Record/mfa.31cf49d7-8a58-4735-b77d-4455463e4dad/Cite) — Gives the exact bilingual title and record identifier for an individual 1912 drawing; the museum page above provides a more accessible displayed image.
- [University of Edinburgh, Patrick Geddes collection: sheet of seven contemporary plans](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — Catalogues a July 1912 press sheet that included Saarinen’s competition plan alongside six other schemes; the catalogue has an image attachment, though it may require requesting a usable scan.
- [NAA series A710 guide](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra) — Establishes that the actual second-prize drawings are held by NAA, rather than merely later redrawings.

## Entry 4 — Donat-Alfred Agache (third prize)

- [NAA: *Discovering France in the NAA*, pages 3–4](https://www.naa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-04/Anne-Marie_Conde-EN.pdf) — Reproduces an image from Agache’s entry (A710/9) and briefly explains the occupational quarters, industrial area, and “people’s palace.”
- [University of Edinburgh: contemporary seven-plan sheet](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — Records a reproduced Agache plan in the top row of a July 1912 press clipping mounted in the Patrick Geddes collection.
- [Centre Pompidou: *La Ville* exhibition inventory](https://cataloguedesexpositions.centrepompidou.fr/La_Ville/Art_et_architecture_en_Europe_1870-1993) — Documents the display of Agache’s Canberra competition proposal, on loan from the Australian archives; a provenance trail rather than a full online image gallery.
- [NAA A710 guide](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra) — Identifies the original third-prize drawings in the archival series.

## Entry 10 — W. Scott Griffiths, R. C. G. Coulter and C. H. Caswell (chairman’s minority-report preference)

- [National Film and Sound Archive: *Designing Canberra: An alternative vision*](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/collection/curated/designing-canberra-an-alternative-vision) — A browsable set of **24 individual glass-slide records** from Griffiths’s illustrated 1916–22 lectures, including his Canberra proposals, government centre, lakes, bridge, drainage and critiques of Griffin; these are later developments and comparative slides, not all 1911 submission sheets.
- [NFSA: illustrated account of the Griffiths slides](https://www.nfsa.gov.au/stories/articles/alternative-canberra-designs-walter-scott-griffiths) — Shows selected slides and Coulter’s cycloramic image with explanatory captions.
- [ABC: Coulter’s coathanger connection](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2014-11-20/coulter-canberra-design/5900804) — Reproduces a **1911 competition watercolour** of the bridge and city and photographs of Coulter’s surviving sketchbook, with commentary on the joint entry.
- [*Axon*: “Suddenly, the Lake”](https://axonjournal.com.au/?p=2344) — Reproduces the submitted lakeside sunset view, specifically credited to NAA **A710/20**, and discusses the entry.
- [NLA catalogue: lithograph of the minority-report choice](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/394464) — Records a 1913 lithograph of the team’s city plan on the survey base map; the catalogue distinguishes this print from the original sheets held by NAA.
- [NAA guide](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra) — Confirms that its A710 series holds the team’s original drawings.

## Ernest Gimson, illustrated by Alfred Hoare Powell (shortlisted)

- [Bridgeman Images: Gimson’s contour-map plan](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/fr/gimson/contour-map-of-site-for-federal-capital-of-australia-plan-of-design-drawn-on-map-1911-pencil/pencil-watercolour/asset/12060816) — Displays the contemporary coloured plan drawn on the site contour map, attributed to Gimson and held by Leicester Museum & Art Gallery; higher-resolution licensing may require payment.
- [Bridgeman Images: Powell’s first competition watercolour](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/en-US/powell-alfred/ernest-gimson-s-competition-entry-to-design-the-new-capital-of-australia-at-canberra-1911-pen-ink/pen-and-ink-with-watercolour-on-paper/asset/1152080) — Shows one original 1911 architectural perspective of Gimson’s imagined city.
- [Bridgeman Images: Powell’s second competition watercolour](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/cn/powell-alfred/ernest-gimson-s-competition-entry-to-design-the-new-capital-of-australia-at-canberra-1911-pen-ink/pen-and-ink-with-watercolour-on-paper/asset/1152081) — A separate catalogue image of another contemporary perspective, rather than a modern visualisation.
- [Bridgeman Images: aerial view of the government buildings](https://www.bridgemanimages.com/de/gimson/aerial-view-of-the-proposed-government-buildings-for-the-federal-capital-of-australia-at-canberra/pencil-watercolour/asset/12060814) — Shows an additional period aerial perspective credited to Gimson’s design and Powell’s illustration.
- [Andrew Cusack: “Ernest Gimson’s Canberra”](https://www.andrewcusack.com/2013/canberra-ernest-gimson/) — A short illustrated blog post that reproduces and comments on the original drawings.
- [The Wilson Museum: Gimson’s architectural designs](https://wilsonmuseum.org.uk/collection/ernest-gimsons-architectural-designs/) — Describes its wider Gimson holdings and Canberra scheme; its illustrated selection is **mostly other projects**, so use the Bridgeman links for actual Canberra images.
- [Canberra Daily: modern reconstruction](https://canberradaily.com.au/ernest-gimsons-lost-vision-for-canberra-domes-and-piazzas/) — Contains several **AI-assisted present-day reconstructions** based on Gimson’s plan and Powell’s paintings, clearly distinct from the 1911 drawings.

## Entry 26 — George Wilson of Knaresborough (returned submission)

- [Rare Maps: photographed original plan](https://www.raremaps.com/gallery/detail/66127jc/canberra-competition-entry-map-of-contour-survey-of-the-si-wilson) — Shows a surviving ink-and-wash design on the original printed contour survey, with close image views, a detailed description and an optional paid high-resolution file; the dealer identifies it as **entry 26** and says it was returned after failing to reach the shortlist.

This is a particularly valuable find because the object surfaced outside the familiar Australian institutional collections. The seller’s attribution and account of its route back to the entrant are the evidence presently available; the page says the object has been sold.

## Entry 9 — Alexander James Macdonald (shortlisted)

- [ANU Archives: Federal Capital Design Competition material](https://archivescollection.anu.edu.au/index.php/federal-capital-design-competition-material) — Catalogues **two 8 × 8 cm lantern slides and two glass negatives** of Macdonald’s plan on the contour survey and a perspective over the administrative centre, naming D. W. Crawford as the perspective’s artist; this is an archival description, not an online scan of all four images.
- [Former *An Ideal City?* entry page](http://www.idealcity.org.au/46/shortlist-09.html) — ANU cites this as the specific historical page for entry 9, but the site did not load during this research; the ANU record above preserves the essential object descriptions.

## Entry 41 — Arthur C. Comey (chairman’s second choice)

- [University of Edinburgh: contemporary seven-plan sheet](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — Catalogues a period printed reproduction of Comey’s plan at the upper left, explicitly labelled “second minority”; the sheet is a way to compare it beside Griffin, Saarinen and Agache.
- [ACT Heritage Library maps and plans list, page 33 of the PDF](https://www.library.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/1365416/map-master-list-alpha-sort.pdf) — Lists a **1913 photocopy** of Comey’s entry among five competition plans, with a local storage reference; it is a holding list, not a downloadable plan image.

“Second minority” refers to the dissenting judge’s ranking, **not** the official second prize, which went to Saarinen.

## Entry 35 — Ernest Schaufelberg, Verner Rees and William Gummer (commended)

- [University of Edinburgh: seven-plan sheet](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — Identifies a contemporary printed version of the London team’s plan in the bottom row; the archive describes an image attachment and the physical mounted clipping.
- [ACT Heritage Library maps and plans list, page 33](https://www.library.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/1365416/map-master-list-alpha-sort.pdf) — Lists a 1913 photocopy of the team’s entry as “highly commended” in a five-plan set; its spelling of Schaufelberg varies.
- [Lutyens Trust: “An Enquiry from Australia”](https://www.lutyenstrust.org.uk/portfolio-item/an-enquiry-from-australia/) — Gives biographical context for the three young designers who had worked for Edwin Lutyens; it does **not** reproduce their Canberra plan.

## Harold Van Buren Magonigle (honourable mention)

- [University of Edinburgh: seven-plan sheet](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — Records a contemporary press reproduction labelled “Hon. Mention” by Magonigle in the bottom row, alongside six other designs.
- [Cornell University Library: Reps’s biography and competition-design page](https://www.library.cornell.edu/Reps/DOCS/magonig.htm) — A historically cited page devoted to the architect’s Canberra design; the old URL did not load in the current search, so the Edinburgh holding is the firmer image lead.

## Bernard Maybeck and collaborators (other entry)

- [Online Archive of California: Maybeck records at UC Berkeley](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/tf9199p16v) — Establishes that Maybeck entered the Canberra competition and describes his wider architectural archive; it is a finding aid, **not a verified online image of his Canberra plan**.
- [John Reps’s *Canberra 1912*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Canberra_1912.html?id=elPo7ti8zNoC) — An illustrated book reproducing preserved competition plans and discussing their designers; check its Maybeck section in a library copy for an image and exact collaborator attribution.

## Other identified designs and collective image leads

- [NLA guide to the Canberra collection](https://www.library.gov.au/research/guides-and-resources/guides-selected-collections/griffin-and-early-canberra-collection) — Explicitly locates Department of Home Affairs photographic copies of the entries at the Library, the best route to lesser-known submissions not individually indexed by search engines.
- [NAA A763 and A710 series descriptions](https://www.naa.gov.au/help-your-research/fact-sheets/walter-burley-griffin-and-design-canberra) — Separates photographs of unsuccessful designs from the four original finalist submissions, allowing targeted archive requests by entrant and series.
- [University of Edinburgh, Patrick Geddes collection](https://archives.collections.ed.ac.uk/repositories/2/archival_objects/182175) — One July 1912 mounted sheet juxtaposes **seven plans**: Comey, Agache, Griffin, the later Departmental Board plan, Saarinen, Schaufelberg–Rees–Gummer and Magonigle.
- [ACT Heritage Library’s five-plan photocopy set](https://www.library.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/1365416/map-master-list-alpha-sort.pdf) — Its catalogue identifies copies of Griffin, Saarinen, Comey, Agache and Schaufelberg–Rees–Gummer at locations A 2 HH 21–25; request the images from the library if those online are insufficient.
- [1995 *An Ideal City?* exhibition](https://mhnsw.au/whats-on/exhibitions/ideal-city-1912-competition-design-canberra/) — Confirms a presentation of 46 surviving designs; the old exhibition site’s individual pages are currently a broken route, but its book is a useful checklist.
- [John W. Reps, *Canberra 1912*](https://books.google.com/books/about/Canberra_1912.html?id=elPo7ti8zNoC) — The most useful illustrated scholarly inventory of the preserved proposals, with reproductions of **47 plans**, rather than a free image site.
- [1912 Federal Capital Designs Board report, ArchivesACT PDF](https://www.archives.act.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/562631/Report_in_Regards_to_Competitive_Designs.pdf) — Contemporary judging report explaining the rankings and the design criteria; useful for matching anonymised entry numbers to the later identified designers.
- [1993 *Planning History* appeal for information](https://planninghistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/1993_vol-15_no1.pdf) — An historical researcher’s list of participants and appeal to locate dispersed originals and descriptions; a discovery aid rather than a gallery.
- [University of Canberra thesis, *Behind the Landscape of Lake Burley Griffin*](https://researchsystem.canberra.edu.au/ws/portalfiles/portal/42140716/Thesis_RR.pdf) — A large illustrated academic PDF with comparative early competition plans, including Saarinen and the Schaufelberg team; its size prevented page-by-page verification in the web viewer.

## A plan that was **not** a competition entry

- [ArchivesACT: the Departmental Board plan](https://www.archives.act.gov.au/find_of_the_month/2013/march/previous_find_of_the_month_-_32013) — Shows and explains the government board’s **post-competition 1912** alternative, often reproduced alongside submissions; it must not be attributed to a separate 1911–12 competitor. The Edinburgh seven-plan sheet includes it for comparison.

### Practical limits

This is an image-finding guide, not an assertion that all 137 designs survive or have been digitised. In particular, a catalogue entry may require an archive request, historical print images can be small, and the 1995 site is inaccessible at the checked URLs. The Gimson reconstruction is explicitly modern; the NFSA Griffiths slides are later illustrated advocacy. For a systematic sweep of all 46 surviving shortlisted plans, use the Reps volume as the checklist, then request the relevant NLA photographs or NAA A763 items by entry number.
