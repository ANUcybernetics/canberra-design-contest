# Significant entries in the 1912 competition: what the National Library holds

> **About this list.**
> It gathers the National Library of Australia's catalogue records for the eight designs named in the 1912 Board report, plus design No. 37 by André Bérard.
> Each record gives its catalogue link and, where the item has been digitised, two image links: the NLA viewer and a direct JPEG.
> The "Notes" are quoted word for word from the catalogue, because they are the only explanatory text the Library holds for most of these plans.
> The records were found by searching the catalogue for the phrase "Competitor no." and for each designer's surname, on 13 November 2026.
> Spelling follows the catalogue, which follows the 1912 report in writing "Schanfelberg" for the London firm.

The Board's report and the Minister's decision are transcribed in [1912-federal-capital-designs-board-report.md](transcriptions/1912-federal-capital-designs-board-report.md).
The plate numbers below refer to that report.

## How the records fit together

Most of these plans survive at the Library in one of three forms.

The first is the **1913 lithograph set**.
After the judging, the New South Wales Department of Lands redrew the eight named designs onto the 1910 contour survey and printed them as lithographs for the Board's report (Parliamentary Paper No. 17, volume 2, 1912).
The Library catalogues the set as a whole, [Bib ID 1917898](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1917898), described as "Lithographs of the winning entries, with the minority report 3 winning entries and 2 commended entries", and then catalogues each sheet on its own.
Each sheet carries a lithograph number, such as "13.132.D.", which identifies it reliably.

The second is **photographs of the original drawings**, made by the Department of Home Affairs for the Board in 1912, or later by the News and Information Bureau.
The originals themselves are, in most cases, "held by the Australian Archives", now the National Archives of Australia.

The third is a small number of **original drawings and lantern slides**.
The most striking is design No. 10, whose hand-coloured original, 133 centimetres square, is in the Library's own collection.

All the 1912 and 1913 items are recorded by the Library as out of copyright.

### No. 29 — Walter Burley Griffin

*Placed first by Smith and Kirkpatrick, and first by the Minister, King O'Malley.*
*Report plates 6 (general plan), 6A (panorama from Mount Ainslie) and 6B ("Provision for extension").*

#### Lithograph on the contour survey, 1913

- **Catalogue:** [Bib ID 1893806](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1893806)
- **Title:** [Federal Capital Design Competition premiated designs]. [Competitor no. 29, Walter Burley Griffin] [cartographic material] : [on base map] Map of contour survey of the site for the Federal Capital of Australia / drawn and printed at the Department of Lands, NSW 1913
- **Published:** Sydney : Dept. of Lands, 1913. 1 map ; 66 x 67 cm.
- **Scale:** ca. 1:9,600
- **Lithograph no.:** 13.132.D.
- **Images:** the Library holds two digitised copies.
  - Copy A: [viewer](https://nla.gov.au/nla.obj-245514574) · [JPEG](https://nla.gov.au/nla.obj-245514574/image)
  - Copy B: [viewer](https://nla.gov.au/nla.obj-230572483) · [JPEG](https://nla.gov.au/nla.obj-230572483/image)
- **Cited in:** John W. Reps, *Canberra 1912: plans and planners of the Australian Capital Competition* (Melbourne University Press, 1997), pp. 140–146.
- **Collection:** Eric Milton Nicholls special map collection.

> Lithograph of Walter Burley Griffin's winning design for Canberra showing his plan on a base map.
> Though this was a first design map drawn in Chicago & based on a standard kit, it demonstrates the main design features of his later maps.
> The Land Axis from Mt. Ainslie to Mt. Bimberi forms the main design focus of the city crossed at right angles by the Water Axis [corresponding to Kings Avenue] along which followed the flood plain of the Molonglo River but which Griffin proposed to create three interconnected lakes (east, central, and west basins) with their shorelines marked by circular or straight promenades or boundary drives.
> The other design axis at right angles to the Land Axis corresponded to Constitution Avenue and formed the base of the National Triangle [referred to now as the Parliamentary Triangle] which here almost encompasses Capital Hill.
> He then based three primary city centres on the three apexes of this triangle.
> Griffin proposed that government buildings ought to be built on each side of the land axis on the south side of a proposed lake, rising up in a hierarchical manner towards a focal point on Capital Hill.
> This would form the Government Centre.
> The base of Capital Hill became the focus of nine intersecting avenues and form a circular, radial network of roads intended to reduce dangerously acute intersections and odd-shaped blocks - this type of layout is reproduced in other city centres.
> The Civic Centre [now called Civic] was placed at the northern apex of the triangle with City Hall located here to carry out administrative functions along with the post office, courts, banks, and places of business aligned along the third axis of Constitution Avenue to act as the city's high street.
> The black dotted line represents the railway line with the railway station located on the eastern tip of the national triangle [i.e. the suburb of Russell] forming the third centre, the Market Centre around which the markets and shops were to be located with the railway line passing underground to avoid disruptions.
> Other more ancillary city centres were grouped around these three axial lines such as the Education Centre established at the western end of the Water Axis with the university at the foot of Black Mountain which included botanical gardens.
> Griffin's modular design is based on these specialised Centres marked on the map as hexagonal or octagonal hubs of intersecting street systems and interconnected by larger radial avenues or boulevards.
> "No. of lith. 13.132.D."
> Compiled for the Federal Capital Designs Board.
> Relates to: Federal Capital City / Federal Capital Designs Board (P.P. no. 17, v.2, 1912).
> National Library's copy at MAP NIC 9 has vertical tear down centre.

#### The designers' own papers

Two manuscript collections at the Library tell this team's side of the story.

- **Papers of Charles Henry Caswell, 1909–1975**, MS 4508: [Bib ID 1341744](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1341744).
  Its centrepiece is an album titled "History of the Design no.10 entered in the competition for the best design of the Federal City of Canberra by R. C. G. Coulter, W. Scott Griffiths, Chas H. Caswell", with a five-page typed history of the design, correspondence with Coane, O'Malley and Arthur Comey, and copies of the designs.
  The catalogue says the design "was awarded fourth place in the competition, although a minority report recommended it for first prize."
- **Papers of W. Scott Griffiths, 1912–1950**, MS 9519: [Bib ID 1748068](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1748068).
  Mostly newspaper clippings, with photographs and watercolours.

Neither collection is digitised.

## Gaps

- **Perspectives and views.** None of the perspectives reproduced as report plates 6A, 6B, 7A, 8A, 9A or 11A turned up in the catalogue as digitised images. The originals are presumably with the National Archives.
- **Competitors' written statements.** The Library catalogue has no written statements by the competitors from the competition itself; Griffin's well-known *Report explanatory of the preliminary general plan* ([Bib ID 1775712](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1775712)) dates from 1913, after the competition.
- **Newspapers.** Trove's newspaper search was not used here, because its API needs a key. The 1912 Melbourne and Sydney papers described the exhibited designs at length and would be the next place to look for contemporary explanation.
