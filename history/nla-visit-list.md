# What to get at the National Library next visit

This is everything we know we want from the Library, gathered from [1912-significant-entries-nla.md](1912-significant-entries-nla.md), [curtis/README.md](curtis/README.md) and the georeferencing work in [georef/](../georef/README.md).
Each item gives its catalogue record (Bib ID), which is enough for staff to find it.
Map items usually have to be requested ahead of a visit; the "Request" button on each catalogue record does that.

The list runs in order of how much each item would help.

## Not at the Library

Most of the original drawings, and the perspectives reproduced as the report's plates, are "held by the Australian Archives", now the National Archives of Australia.
That is a separate trip: the NAA reading room is also in Canberra.

## Scanning notes

These keep the new scans ready for the georeferencing pipeline.

- **Capture the whole sheet, edges and all.** The pipeline finds the 2,500 ft grid lines, the neatline and the Ainslie corner, so nothing on the sheet should be cropped off.
- **Lay it flat and light it evenly.** Glare and shadow hide the faint grid lines; the existing lithographs' lines are only a few grey levels darker than the paper.
- **Aim for about 400 dpi at the sheet's real size.** For a 66 cm lithograph that is roughly 10,000 pixels across, about what the Library's own masters are.
- **If an item is too big for one shot, overlap the pieces by a good margin,** and keep one wide shot of the whole sheet as a guide.
- **Put a ruler or colour card in frame** if you are photographing rather than scanning.
- **Name the files `1912-design-NN-designer-what-bibID`** and put them in `history/scans/1912-plans/`, like the others.
- **Check the copying rules with staff on the day.** The 1912 and 1913 items are recorded as out of copyright, but the manuscript collections may have their own conditions.
