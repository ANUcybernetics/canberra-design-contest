# Curtis on the Federal Capital designs

On 20 August 1912, L. A. Curtis, President of the Institution of Surveyors, N.S.W., read a paper called "The Federal Capital Designs" to the Institution in Sydney.
He had seen the entries when they were shown at the Sydney Town Hall for a few days in July.
His verdict was blunt: the prizes had gone to pretty drawings, and the most practical plan, No. 10, had been passed over.
In his words, "the best workable design is relegated to the background, while the impracticable one gets the prize."

The paper was printed in full in *The Surveyor* of 31 August 1912, followed by a reply from Charles H. Caswell, one of the three authors of No. 10.
This folder lists every design Curtis names and the National Library of Australia's records for each.
Tom Griffiths has transcribed the whole article, Caswell's reply included, in [../transcriptions/1912-curtis-the-federal-capital-designs.md](../transcriptions/1912-curtis-the-federal-capital-designs.md).

## How the numbers line up

Curtis uses the competition's own arbitrary design numbers, the same numbers the Board's report uses and the Library catalogues as "Competitor no.".
They agree wherever both can be checked: his No. 53, "entitled the city of the Southern Cross", is the Library's "City of the Southern Cross. [Competitor no. 53, R.J.A. Roberts]".

When he writes of "Nos. 1 and 3" and "No. 2" in his paragraph on design 4, he means the first, second and third prizes, not designs 1, 2 and 3.

## The designs he discusses

Curtis names 33 designs.
The Library holds records for 25 of them, and has digitised at least one image for 17.

Each entry gives Curtis's own words, the designer as the Library records them, and the Library's records.
"Viewer" opens the NLA's zoomable viewer; "JPEG" fetches the image directly (up to 5,000 pixels).
The full-resolution TIFF of any digitised item is at `https://nla.gov.au/tarkine/nla.obj-<id>/m`.
Items marked "not digitised" can be ordered through the Library's Copies Direct service.

## Things worth checking

- **Numbers above 137.** The Library's record for George Wilson's entry (No. 26, [Bib ID 8176700](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/8176700)) cites John Reps for a total of 137 entries, yet Curtis names Nos. 139 and 140. Either the numbering ran past the count, or one of the figures is wrong. Reps, *Canberra 1912*, should settle it.
- **No. 15 is Ernest Gimson.** The Library catalogues No. 15 under "Ernest W. Ginson", but it is the entry of Ernest Gimson (1864–1919), the English Arts and Crafts architect. Gimson's written report survives as a typescript among his papers and drawings at the Cheltenham Art Gallery & Museum, and John Reps transcribed it for Cornell (now only on the [Wayback Machine](https://web.archive.org/web/2013/http://www.library.cornell.edu/Reps/DOCS/gimson.htm)). The report says his lakeside arcade is "shewn in the two views", and that the stadium on the north shore "is reached by a bridge of thirteen arches"; the Library's digitised sheet for No. 15 ([1967689](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/1967689)) is exactly that pair of views, with the arched bridge and stadium in the foreground. It is titled "Competitive designs for the Federal Capital of Australia". Gimson planned a compact city of about 25,000 people on the south bank, which is why Curtis found it covered "only a small portion of the area". His plan itself ([445225](https://catalogue.nla.gov.au/catalog/445225)) is not digitised. A full-resolution copy of the views is kept locally at `../scans/1912-plans/1912-design-15-gimson-views-nla.obj-233376352.tif`, with a thumbnail at [`../thumbnails/1912-plans/1912-design-15-gimson-views-nla.obj-233376352.jpg`](../thumbnails/1912-plans/1912-design-15-gimson-views-nla.obj-233376352.jpg).
- **No. 37's nationality.** Curtis calls Bérard's entry "Another beautiful English design"; the Library records only the name.
- **No. 53 deserves a scan.** Curtis's favourite overlooked plan, the "City of the Southern Cross" by the surveyor R. J. A. Roberts, has never been digitised. It can be ordered from the Library.
- **The source volume.** The photographs do not record which library or collection the copy of *The Surveyor* came from. The newspaper clippings and typed letters at the edges of the photographs, including one that mentions sewerage and drainage, suggest a collection of papers rather than a bound run of the journal; the Caswell papers (MS 4508) are a possibility, but this has not been checked.
